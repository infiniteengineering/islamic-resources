About This Project

Compilation methodology and source standards

About This Collection

This collection has been compiled as a personal effort to gather authentic daily adhkār from the Qur'ān and the Sunnah in a structured and accessible format.

It is not the work of a scholar and is not presented as scholarly authority or formal religious guidance. Rather, it is a personal compilation based on recognised hadith sources and commonly accepted authenticity gradings found in well-known Islamic references.

The material has been organised and reviewed through independent study, supported by digital research tools and AI-assisted organisation. These tools were used only to help structure, cross-reference, and present information drawn from recognised Islamic sources.

No claim is made of independent hadith authentication or scholarly verification. This work relies on the published judgements of recognised hadith scholars where referenced.

Every effort has been made to present accurate and reliable material. However, as a personal compilation, unintentional errors may exist.

Corrections, clarifications, and scholarly feedback are sincerely welcomed.

The intention of this project is personal practice, benefit, and sharing authentic remembrance — not issuing rulings, interpretations, or new religious conclusions.

May Allah forgive any mistakes and accept what is correct.

Compilation Criteria

Reliance on Scholarly Authentication

This collection is based on supplications authenticated by recognised scholars as Ṣaḥīḥ (authentic) or Ḥasan (good). No independent hadith authentication has been performed — this work relies entirely on the grading and verification of established Sunni hadith scholars.

Ṣaḥīḥ (Authentic): Narrations with an unbroken chain of trustworthy narrators with precise memory, free from hidden defects and irregularities.

Ḥasan (Good): Narrations meeting Ṣaḥīḥ criteria except for slight weakness in narrator memory, authenticated by scholars as acceptable for practice.

What was excluded: Supplications graded as weak (Ḍaʿīf), fabricated, or unverified in the published scholarly works referenced.

Scholars Relied Upon

This compilation draws from the authentication work of recognised Sunni hadith scholars, primarily:

  • Imam Muḥammad Nāṣir al-Dīn al-Albānī (1914–1999) — His authentication of hadith collections
  • Sheikh ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Bāz (1910–1999) — Former Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia
  • Imam Yaḥyā ibn Sharaf al-Nawawī (1233–1277) — Classical hadith scholar
  • Contemporary scholars including Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾūṭ and others who verified major hadith collections

The gradings and references provided are based on their published work, not independent verification.

Source Hierarchy

Primary Sources

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim are prioritised as the most authentic hadith collections after the Qur'an. All narrations from these two works are accepted without additional verification needed.

Secondary Sources

The Four Sunan (Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah) and authenticated narrations from Musnad Ahmad, Ṣaḥīḥ Ibn Ḥibbān, and other classical collections are included when authenticated by the scholars mentioned above.

Qur'anic Supplications

Verses from the Qur'an used as supplications (such as Āyat al-Kursī, the last verses of Sūrah al-Baqarah, and the Three Quls) are included with their hadith evidence for when and how the Prophet ﷺ instructed their recitation.

How I Handled Variations

Multiple Authentic Wordings

Where multiple authentic wordings exist for the same supplication (such as the opening duʿā' in prayer, the Tashahhud, or Qunūt of Witr):

  • The most commonly used version found in mainstream adhkar collections has been selected
  • A note has been added stating: "This is one of the authentic wordings reported. Other authentic wordings are also established in the Sunnah."
  • Preference was given to versions appearing frequently in works like Ḥiṣn al-Muslim and similar compilations

Important: Multiple authentic wordings do not weaken each other. Each is a valid transmission from the Prophet ﷺ. The selection of one version over another is simply for practical organization — not a judgment that one is "better" than another.

Frequency & Timing

Where different reports exist about numbers or timing, what appeared most widely practiced in mainstream Sunni communities has been selected, based on the scholars' authentication. These are compilation choices, not scholarly rulings.

Reference Format

Citation Structure

Every supplication includes full references in the format:

Source — Hadith Number — Grading

Examples:

  • Al-Bukhari 6405 — Ṣaḥīḥ
  • Muslim 2723 — Ṣaḥīḥ
  • Abu Dawud 5082 — Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)
  • At-Tirmidhi 3575 — Ḥasan Ṣaḥīḥ

This allows independent verification by anyone who wishes to check the sources.

Source Type Labels

Each supplication is labelled with its source type:

  • QUR'AN: Verses from the Qur'an
  • PROPHETIC SUPPLICATION: Duʿā' taught by the Prophet ﷺ in his words
  • PRACTICE OF THE PROPHET ﷺ: Actions or habits of the Prophet ﷺ with instruction or benefit noted

Scholarly Framework

Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah

This collection follows the understanding and methodology of Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah — the mainstream Sunni tradition represented by the vast majority of Muslims throughout history.

We adhere to the principle: No innovation in worship. All supplications are traced back to the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah, with no additions from personal preference, dreams, or unsourced practices.

Limitations

This compilation has the following limitations:

  • No qualification to perform independent hadith authentication
  • Relies entirely on published work of recognised scholars
  • Organised with AI assistance for structuring and cross-referencing
  • May contain errors in selection, translation, or presentation
  • Represents commonly used adhkar, not an exhaustive collection
  • Personal compilation choices, not scholarly determinations

Commitment: If errors are found or better information becomes available, corrections will be made. May Allah forgive any mistakes and accept what is correct.

What This Is NOT

This collection is NOT:

  • Scholarly work — I am a lay Muslim, not a scholar of hadith or fiqh
  • Original research — I have compiled from existing authenticated sources
  • Legal rulings — Consult qualified scholars for religious questions
  • The only valid versions — Other authentic wordings exist and are equally valid
  • Exhaustive — This focuses on commonly used authenticated supplications
  • Free from error — As a human effort, mistakes may exist despite my best efforts

What this IS: A sincere attempt by a lay Muslim to organize well-known, authenticated daily adhkar in an accessible format for personal benefit and sharing.

Independent Verification

Verify Everything

We encourage users to verify any supplication by:

  • Checking the hadith reference provided
  • Consulting recognized hadith collections online (such as sunnah.com)
  • Reading the authentication notes of scholars like al-Albānī
  • Asking qualified scholars if uncertain about any content

The Qur'an commands: "O you who believe, if there comes to you a disobedient one with information, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance..." (49:6)

This applies to all religious knowledge — even ours.

Contact & Corrections

If you find any errors, have questions about sources, or wish to suggest improvements based on authentic scholarship, we welcome respectful scholarly feedback.

May Allah accept this effort and make it a source of benefit for Muslims seeking to remember Him in the manner taught by His Messenger ﷺ.
