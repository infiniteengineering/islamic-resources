Section One

Qurʾānic Mentions

Idrīs (عليه السلام) is mentioned by name twice in the Noble Qurʾān, across two surahs: Sūrah Maryam (19:56–57) and Sūrah al-Anbiyāʾ (21:85–86). Though brief, both passages carry significant theological weight — establishing his rank as a prophet, his quality of truthfulness, his patience, and the divine honour of his elevation.

His Rank and the Honourable Station

وَٱذْكُرْ فِى ٱلْكِتَابِ إِدْرِيسَ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ صِدِّيقًا نَّبِيًّا ۝ وَرَفَعْنَاهُ مَكَانًا عَلِيًّا
Wa-dhkur fī l-kitābi Idrīsa · innahu kāna ṣiddīqan nabiyyā · wa-rafaʿnāhu makānan ʿaliyyā
"And mention in the Book ˹the story of˺ Idrīs. He was truly a man of truth and a prophet. And We raised him to an honourable position."
Sūrah Maryam (19:56–57) — The Clear Qurʾān

Allāh describes Idrīs with two titles: ṣiddīq (a man of truth — one whose inner conviction and outward conduct are in complete alignment) and nabī (prophet). The word ṣiddīq is among the highest descriptions Allāh gives to a human being in the Qurʾān, also used for Ibrāhīm (عليه السلام) and Maryam. The phrase makānan ʿaliyyan (an honourable position) is discussed below under Scholarly Differences.

Scholarly Difference — The Meaning of "Raised to an Honourable Position"
  • Scholars of tafsīr differed on the meaning of rafaʿnāhu makānan ʿaliyyan (We raised him to an honourable position). Ibn Kathīr reports several positions: the majority understood it as a reference to a high rank and station with Allāh — meaning spiritual and prophetic elevation. Others, drawing on the Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ encountered Idrīs in the fourth heaven, understood it as a reference to a physical elevation to the heavens. A third position holds it refers to his being placed in Paradise during his lifetime. Ibn Kathīr himself notes that the hadith of the Night Journey supports the view of a bodily elevation. Allāh knows best.

Among the Patient and the Righteous

وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِدْرِيسَ وَذَا ٱلْكِفْلِ ۖ كُلٌّ مِّنَ ٱلصَّابِرِينَ ۝ وَأَدْخَلْنَاهُمْ فِى رَحْمَتِنَآ ۖ إِنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلصَّالِحِينَ
Wa-Ismāʿīla wa-Idrīsa wa-dhā l-Kifl · kullun mina ṣ-ṣābirīn · wa-adkhalnāhum fī raḥmatinā · innahum mina ṣ-ṣāliḥīn
"And ˹remember˺ Ishmael, Idrīs, and Dul-Kifl. They were all steadfast. And We admitted them into Our mercy. They were truly of the righteous."
Sūrah al-Anbiyāʾ (21:85–86) — The Clear Qurʾān

Here Idrīs is grouped with Ismāʿīl and Dhul-Kifl among those praised for ṣabr (steadfast patience). The Qurʾān confirms three things about him in this passage: he was patient in his trial, he was admitted into Allāh's mercy, and he was among the righteous (ṣāliḥīn). Scholars note that ṣabr here encompasses perseverance in obedience, patience in trial, and restraint from what is prohibited.

Section Two

Life & Story ⌄

The Qurʾān speaks of Idrīs (عليه السلام) briefly but with great honour. What is established through the Qurʾān and authentic hadith is his prophethood, his truthfulness, his patience, his admission into divine mercy, and his elevation to a high station. The details of his life and mission beyond this are drawn from early Islamic historical reports, which are presented clearly labelled below.

Identity and Lineage

Idrīs (عليه السلام) is a prophet of Allāh whose name appears in the Qurʾān. Many early scholars of Islamic history held that he was a descendant of Shīth (Seth), son of Ādam (عليه السلام), and that he lived before Nūḥ (عليه السلام) — making him among the earliest prophets in the chain of prophethood that began with Ādam. This position is widely transmitted in early historical literature, though the precise lineage details are not established through revelation. His name in Arabic, Idrīs, is understood by some scholars to be derived from the Arabic root meaning to study or to teach, though this is a linguistic observation rather than an established fact.

Scholarly Difference — Lineage and Historical Placement
  • Scholars differed on the precise lineage of Idrīs (عليه السلام). The most widely transmitted position in early Islamic history literature places him as: Idrīs ibn Yard ibn Mahlāʾīl ibn Qaynān ibn Ānūsh ibn Shīth ibn Ādam. However, some classical scholars noted uncertainty in the intermediate links of this chain. His placement before Nūḥ (عليه السلام) is widely transmitted in early Islamic historical literature and adopted by many classical scholars of history and tafsīr, though some placed him after Nūḥ. Allāh knows best.

Mission and Message

Like all prophets, the foundational message of Idrīs (عليه السلام) was tawḥīd — the worship of Allāh alone, without partner or intermediary. The Qurʾān's description of him as a ṣiddīq and nabī establishes that he received divine revelation and conveyed it faithfully. Classical scholars understood his mission to be directed at the descendants of Ādam in the generations following Shīth, at a time when human societies were still in their early formation and the message of tawḥīd needed to be renewed and carried forward.

The Qurʾān's praise of his ṣabr (patience) in Sūrah al-Anbiyāʾ indicates that his mission was not without difficulty. Patience in the Qurʾānic sense — as scholars explain — encompasses perseverance in calling to Allāh despite opposition, steadfastness in personal obedience, and restraint in the face of hardship. That Allāh specifically commends him for this quality suggests his prophetic work involved sustained effort and endurance.

The Elevation to a High Station

The most theologically significant element of Idrīs's story is the Qurʾānic statement: "We raised him to an honourable position" (19:57). The hadith of the Night Journey and Ascension (Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj) establishes that the Prophet ﷺ encountered Idrīs (عليه السلام) in the fourth heaven. Many scholars understood this as supporting the Qurʾānic statement that Allāh raised him to an honourable position — the hadith providing a concrete context for what the verse describes.

Ibn Kathīr (رحمه الله) notes that the elevation of Idrīs is described by scholars as a sign of Allāh's favour upon him: he was singled out by name in the Qurʾān, described as truthful and a prophet, praised for his patience, admitted into Allāh's mercy, and elevated to a station that the Prophet ﷺ himself witnessed. These verses combine multiple forms of divine commendation — truthfulness, prophethood, patience, righteousness, mercy, and elevation — within a brief Qurʾānic mention.

Scholarly Difference — Did Idrīs Die or Was He Raised Alive?
  • Some early scholars held that Idrīs (عليه السلام) was raised to the heavens alive, analogous to ʿĪsā (عليه السلام), and that he remains alive. Others, including Ibn Kathīr, held that he died — either before his elevation or as part of it — citing the Qurʾānic principle that every soul shall taste death. The authentic hadith of the Isrāʾ does not resolve this question definitively, as the Prophet ﷺ encountered other deceased prophets during the same journey. This remains a matter of scholarly difference. Allāh knows best.

Early Historical Reports — Skills and Knowledge

⚠ Early Historical Reports — Not Established Revelation
  • The following details about Idrīs (عليه السلام) are drawn from early Islamic historical literature — transmitted by scholars such as Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabarī — and are not established by the Qurʾān or ṣaḥīḥ ḥadīth. They should be understood as reported information from early sources, not confirmed religious facts.

Early Islamic historical reports — transmitted in Ibn Kathīr's Qiṣaṣ al-Anbiyāʾ and al-Ṭabarī's Tārīkh — attribute to Idrīs (عليه السلام) several distinctions: that he was among the first to write with a pen, among the first to sew and wear sewn garments, and among the first to study the movements of the stars and the science of arithmetic. These reports are transmitted in early Islamic historical literature and are not established through the Qurʾān or authentic Sunnah. They present him as a figure through whom Allāh conveyed knowledge and practical wisdom to early humanity — continuing the pattern of divine guidance that began with Ādam (عليه السلام).

Identification with Enoch — Isrāʾīliyyāt

⚠ Isrāʾīliyyāt — Reported from Earlier Traditions
  • Some classical Muslim scholars — including Ibn Kathīr — noted that Idrīs (عليه السلام) has been identified by earlier traditions with the biblical figure of Enoch (mentioned in Genesis as a man who "walked with God" and was "taken by God"). Others noted parallels with Hermes Trismegistus in Hellenistic tradition. These identifications are drawn from Judeo-Christian and other external sources and do not have a Qurʾānic or ṣaḥīḥ ḥadīth basis. These identifications arise from external historical traditions and are not part of the Islamic transmitted knowledge regarding Idrīs. They are mentioned here only as transmitted historical comparisons. They do not form any part of the Islamic theological understanding of Idrīs (عليه السلام), and no doctrinal conclusions may be drawn from them. Allāh knows best.

Death and Legacy

The manner and timing of the death of Idrīs (عليه السلام) is among the points of scholarly difference noted above. What is established is that he was among the earliest prophets, that he carried the message of tawḥīd to his generation, that he was praised by Allāh in the Qurʾān as truthful, patient, and righteous, and that he was elevated to a station that the Prophet ﷺ himself witnessed during the Night Journey. His legacy in the chain of prophethood is that of a link between Ādam and Nūḥ (عليهما السلام) — one who renewed the call to Allāh in a generation that needed it.

Section Three

Authenticated Aḥādīth

Direct narrations specifically concerning Idrīs (عليه السلام) are limited in the hadith corpus. The most significant is the account of the Night Journey and Ascension (Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj), which is established in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. All ḥadīth numbers are linked to sunnah.com for independent verification.

The Encounter with Idrīs During the Night Journey

Mālik ibn Saʿsaʿah (رضي الله عنه) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ described his Night Journey, saying: "…Then we ascended to the fourth heaven. Gabriel sought permission to enter. It was asked, 'Who is it?' He replied, 'Gabriel.' It was asked, 'And who is with you?' He said, 'Muḥammad.' It was asked, 'Has he been sent for?' He replied, 'Yes.' The gate was then opened and I came upon Idrīs. He welcomed me and invoked Allāh's blessings upon me. Allāh says of him: 'And We raised him to an honourable position.'"
Narrated by Mālik ibn Saʿsaʿah (رضي الله عنه), as reported by Anas ibn Mālik (رضي الله عنه)
Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3207 · Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 164a
Scholarly Note — Idrīs in the Fourth Heaven
  • The placement of Idrīs (عليه السلام) in the fourth heaven is understood by scholars to be consistent with the Qurʾānic statement that Allāh raised him to a high station. Ibn Kathīr notes that the Prophet ﷺ encountering him there — rather than in the lower heavens where other prophets were placed — is itself a marker of his elevated rank. The hadith quotes the Qurʾānic verse directly (19:57) as the explanation for why Idrīs was there, making it one of the most direct connections between Qurʾānic description and prophetic narration concerning Idrīs.
Section Four

Lessons & Reflections

Key lessons drawn from the life of Idrīs (عليه السلام), in accordance with the understanding of Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah.

🌿 Key Lessons from Idrīs (عليه السلام)
  1. Truthfulness (ṣidq) is among the highest human qualities. Allāh describes Idrīs (عليه السلام) as a ṣiddīq before describing him as a prophet — placing truthfulness as the character foundation from which prophethood flows. The scholars explain that ṣidq encompasses truthfulness in speech, truthfulness in intention, and truthfulness between the inner state and the outer conduct. It is a quality Allāh specifically commends, and one the Prophet ﷺ identified as the path to righteousness and Paradise.
  2. Allāh elevates those who are patient and righteous. The Qurʾānic account of Idrīs teaches that ṣabr and ṣalāḥ (righteousness) are not without reward. Allāh specifically records His elevation of Idrīs as an act of divine honour — a response to a life of truthfulness, patience, and prophetic faithfulness. The believer should understand that no act of sincere obedience is ever lost or forgotten by Allāh.
  3. The chain of prophethood demonstrates the continuity of divine guidance. Idrīs stands as a link between Ādam and Nūḥ (عليهما السلام) — evidence that Allāh did not leave humanity without guidance after the descent from Paradise. In every era and generation, Allāh sent a messenger to renew the message of tawḥīd. This continuity is itself a mercy — and a proof that human beings have never been abandoned by their Creator.
  4. Divine commendation is not measured by length of account. Idrīs receives only two Qurʾānic passages — yet within them, Allāh calls him truthful, a prophet, patient, righteous, admitted into mercy, and elevated to an honourable station. The brevity of the Qurʾānic account does not diminish his rank. This teaches that Allāh's recognition of a person's deeds is not proportional to their public prominence or the volume of their story.
  5. Patience in the call to Allāh is a mark of the prophets. That Idrīs is grouped in Sūrah al-Anbiyāʾ with Ismāʿīl and Dhul-Kifl specifically for their ṣabr teaches that prophetic work inherently involves difficulty and resistance. The sincere believer who encounters hardship in calling to good or maintaining obedience finds in Idrīs (and all the prophets) a model of perseverance that Allāh Himself has preserved in His Book.
  6. Epistemic humility is required where revelation is silent. The details of Idrīs's life — his lineage, the nature of his elevation, whether he died — involve genuine scholarly disagreement precisely because the Qurʾān and authentic hadith do not specify them. The correct response to such uncertainty is not speculation but restraint: to affirm what Allāh has established, acknowledge what is disputed, and close with "Allāh knows best." This is the methodology of classical Sunni scholarship and the only approach consistent with intellectual honesty.
Section Five

Supplications (Duʿāʾ)

No specific supplication is authentically attributed to Idrīs (عليه السلام) in the Qurʾān or ṣaḥīḥ ḥadīth beyond the universal prophetic supplications for guidance and forgiveness. The Qurʾān does not record a personal duʿāʾ from him in the manner it does for Ādam, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, and others.

What the Qurʾān does record is Allāh's description of Idrīs as a ṣiddīq — one of complete truthfulness. Scholars note that the foundation of all supplication is the alignment of the heart with what the tongue says — precisely the quality of ṣidq. The story of Idrīs therefore calls the believer toward sincerity in their own duʿāʾ, even without a specific text attributed to him.
