About This Section

The Qurʾān mentions twenty-five prophets by name. Each was sent by Allāh (سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى) to guide a particular people — delivering the same core message: worship Allāh alone, avoid shirk, and live righteously. Their stories are a source of guidance, patience, and certainty for the believers.

Each prophet's page contains a full narrative drawn from Ibn Kathīr's Qiṣaṣ al-Anbiyāʾ, the relevant Qurʾānic mentions with Arabic text and translation, authenticated aḥādīth with full grading references, and a lessons panel summarising the key takeaways — all in accordance with Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah.

Only ṣaḥīḥ and ḥasan narrations are used. All ḥadīth are linked to sunnah.com for independent verification. Weak or fabricated narrations are excluded entirely.

📖 Prophets & Messengers — An Important Distinction

In Islamic theology, Allāh sent two categories of chosen men to guide humanity: the nabī (نبي — prophet) and the rasūl (رسول — messenger). Understanding the difference between them is part of the correct Sunni ʿaqīdah.

A nabī is a man whom Allāh selected and granted revelation — guiding his people in accordance with divine instruction. Many scholars explain that the nabī continues and upholds an existing revealed law among his people.

A rasūl is also a prophet, but scholars explain that he is sent with a distinct mission — such as delivering a new divine law, calling a people who have rejected guidance, or conveying revelation publicly to a community. Every rasūl is a nabī — but not every nabī is a rasūl.

The Qurʾān distinguishes between prophets and messengers, and scholars of ʿaqīdah — including Ibn Kathīr and Ibn al-Qayyim — discussed the differences between their roles.

The twenty-five prophets named in the Qurʾān include both categories. Among them, scholars identify confirmed messengers — including Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā, and Muḥammad (ﷺ) — each of whom was sent with a distinct mission and message. The remaining named prophets carried the message forward within an existing law. Allāh knows best regarding those whose precise designation is not explicitly specified.

Where revelation is silent, we remain silent.
